From a Restless Kid to a Water Prodigy

Michael Fred Phelps II was born on June 30, 1985, in Baltimore, Maryland. As a child he struggled to sit still — he was diagnosed with ADHD at the age of nine, and swimming became the outlet his mother, Debbie, and his coaches turned to as a way to channel that boundless energy.

At the North Baltimore Aquatic Club, coach Bob Bowman spotted something different in the lanky nine-year-old: an almost inhuman wingspan, oversized hands and feet, and a hunger to win that never seemed to run dry. By age 15, Phelps had already qualified for the 2000 Sydney Olympics — the youngest male swimmer to make a U.S. Olympic team in 68 years.

He didn't medal in Sydney. But the disappointment lit a fire. Four years later in Athens, he arrived a different swimmer altogether.

"I want to be known as the guy who changed the sport of swimming."
— Michael Phelps

A Career Measured in Lane Lines

Five Olympic Games. A story that kept rewriting the record books.

01

Athens 2004 — The Breakthrough

At just 19, Phelps won 6 gold and 2 bronze medals, tying the record for most medals won at a single Olympics by an American. The swimming world took notice.

02

Beijing 2008 — Eight Gold, One Games

Phelps did what no Olympian ever had: 8 gold medals in a single Games, breaking Mark Spitz's 1972 record of 7. The margin in the 100m butterfly final was one hundredth of a second — the closest possible finish in swimming.

03

London 2012 — Most Decorated Ever

With 4 more gold and 2 silver medals, Phelps became the most decorated Olympian in history, surpassing gymnast Larisa Latynina's total of 18 medals. He retired from the sport — for the first time.

04

Rio 2016 — The Comeback

Two years after retirement, fatherhood and a renewed sense of purpose pulled Phelps back into the pool. At 31, he won 5 more gold medals and 1 silver, closing his Olympic career with a farewell victory lap around the world he had transformed.

★

The Final Tally

28 Olympic medals — 23 of them gold — across five Games. No athlete in the history of the modern Olympics has come close.

Moments That Defined a Legend

Beyond the Pool

Mental Health Advocate

After retirement, Phelps became one of the most prominent athletes to speak openly about depression and anxiety, using his platform to encourage others to seek help.

The Michael Phelps Foundation

Founded in 2008, the foundation promotes water safety and healthy, active lifestyles for children, teaching countless kids to swim.

An Enduring Blueprint

His training discipline, race-day rituals, and relentless work ethic are still studied by coaches and athletes across every sport, not just swimming.
